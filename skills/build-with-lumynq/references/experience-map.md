# LumynQ Experience Map

Use this map for a new journey or a material redesign. Adapt the depth to the stakes and scope. Emotional states are hypotheses unless research directly supports them.

## 1. Frame the transformation

- **Experience scope:** The journey, actor, entry point, and end condition.
- **Practical job:** What the person is trying to accomplish.
- **Human outcome:** What the person should understand, decide, regain, express, or become able to do.
- **Starting condition:** Relevant emotional, cognitive, social, and practical context.
- **Supported shift:** A defensible movement such as uncertain → oriented, exposed → protected, overwhelmed → able to take one next step, or unheard → accurately represented.
- **Anti-goal:** A condition the design must not create, such as pressured compliance, false reassurance, shame, dependence, or hidden loss of control.

Do not define success as making everyone happy, calm, loyal, or engaged. Define it as supporting a meaningful capability or state while respecting agency.

## 2. Record assumptions

For each consequential assumption, capture:

| Assumption | Evidence | Confidence | Risk if wrong | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What may be true | Observed—live, Observed—code, Observed—plan, Inferred, or Unknown | Low, medium, or high | Human or product consequence | Interview, test, analytics, or operational check |

Prioritize assumptions about vulnerability, intent, comprehension, consequence, culture, access, and privacy.

## 3. Map the moments that matter

Include only moments relevant to the journey:

- arrival and orientation;
- disclosure or data entry;
- consequential choice;
- commitment, submission, payment, or consent;
- waiting and uncertainty;
- empty or incomplete state;
- warning, denial, or permission failure;
- error and correction;
- cancellation or exit;
- completion and what happens next;
- return, continuity, or re-entry.

For each moment, define:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| User action | What are they trying to do here? |
| Likely state | What may they feel, believe, fear, or misunderstand? |
| Human need | What do they need to understand, control, protect, or accomplish? |
| System responsibility | What must the product do—not merely say? |
| Visible response | What behavior, interaction, and language makes that responsibility legible? |
| Recovery | If this fails or the inference is wrong, how can the person correct, exit, or continue? |

## 4. Define the trust contract

State:

- what the system knows and how it knows it;
- what it is inferring and with what consequence;
- what data is necessary, optional, retained, or shared;
- what the user can inspect, correct, undo, decline, or delete;
- where a human can intervene;
- what the system cannot promise.

When intelligence influences a consequential outcome, favor traceable evidence and correction over unexplained confidence.

## 5. Design the response stack

Specify all four layers:

1. **Behavior:** processing, timing, safeguards, defaults, routing, and escalation.
2. **Interaction:** choices, visibility, confirmation, correction, undo, and exit.
3. **Language:** clear, emotionally appropriate, non-performative copy.
4. **Learning:** signals that show whether the experience created clarity, agency, recovery, or a trustworthy next step.

## 6. Validate the shift

Use evidence such as successful correction, informed completion, reduced abandonment at a confusing state, accurate expectation-setting, recovery without support, user-reported understanding, or an observed ability to make the intended decision.

Do not optimize emotional outcomes through surveillance or infer them from engagement alone.
